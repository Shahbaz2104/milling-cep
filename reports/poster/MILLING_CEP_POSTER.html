Detecting dangerous milling conditions from vibration and current

01The engineering problem

Radial depth of cut (RDOC) controls how much of the cutter engages the workpiece. At 8 mm on a 10 mm cutter, chip load and cutting force rise; chatter, overload, accelerated wear and breakage become likely. The operator knows the programmed setting — a process monitor does not.

Task: binary classification of each cycle as optimal (RDOC 4.5 mm) or non-optimal (8 mm) from 120 accelerometer and current statistics. Metadata, hardness and wear counters are excluded from the inputs.

Operational use: a decision-support alarm. A missed dangerous cut risks the tool and spindle; a false alarm costs an inspection. The threshold is set accordingly.

03The trap: perfect confounding

0 experiments and 0 tools contain both classes. A random row split puts the same tool in training and test — it memorises tool identity. Random-split ROC-AUC 0.998; tool-grouped 0.942.

04Leakage-safe method

1
Clean and audit. Parse export quirks, cross-check both sources, keep all 968 cycles.
2
Features. signed-log1p on 120 sensor statistics; balanced class weights.
3
Grouped CV. Five tool folds; hyperparameters tuned inside each outer fold.
4
Compare. Dummy, logistic regression, random forest, histogram gradient boosting.
5
Select and stress-test. Threshold on out-of-fold probabilities; leave-one-tool-out and two held-out experiments.

07Where the errors are

Residual risk: an optimal tool can look dangerous when worn. A tool-specific early-life baseline is the most promising fix.

08It reads force, not the answer key

  • Drive-axis mean acceleration is the top signal — a static force offset grows with radial engagement.
  • Drive and spindle current variability follows the heavier, more interrupted cut.
  • No identifier, hardness or wear counter is in the feature matrix.

10Engineering interpretation

A larger radial engagement raises the chip load, cutting force, and therefore drive-axis mean acceleration and current variability. The forest combines these correlated channels into a boundary that catches 96% of dangerous cycles. The errors are equally physical: wear, hardness and tool geometry push the signature in the same direction as RDOC, which is why one optimal tool overlaps the dangerous class.

Consequence: a false negative continues a dangerous cut; a false positive costs an inspection. Deploy as a human-in-the-loop alarm, not an automatic stop.

11Caveats and next steps

Limitations

  • RDOC confounded with tool, material and wear stage.
  • 14 tools, 5 dangerous; tool 7 dominates errors.
  • Pre-computed features; raw waveforms unavailable.
  • Raw ADC units, one machine, one campaign.
  • Threshold is a policy choice.

Recommendations

  • Require two consecutive flagged cycles.
  • Add a tool-specific early-life baseline.
  • Run a balanced experiment at both RDOC levels.
  • Calibrate ADC to g and A before transfer.
  • Tune the threshold to inspection vs scrap cost.
